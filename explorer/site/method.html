Background

Method & data

How the Cross-Trait Aggregation Score (CTAS) is constructed from a 16-trait cardiovascular panel, how significance and the nested q-value sets are defined, and what every field in a locus card means.

Approach

1

What CTAS is

The Cross-Trait Aggregation Score (CTAS) is a single per-gene statistic that measures how much a gene's aggregate association evidence across a panel of 16 Pan-UK Biobank cardiovascular traits exceeds what the panel's own correlation structure would already predict. CTAS places the dependence correction inside the gene-level statistic: it whitens each gene's cross-trait evidence using the transformed trait correlation matrix, keeps the positive residual evidence, and combines its magnitude and its effective breadth across decorrelated dimensions.

Thereafter this page uses "CTAS" or "the CTAS statistic". CTAS is a prioritiser of cross-trait-relevant loci; it is not itself a test of a shared causal variant or a causal-gene assignment.

2

What the Explorer shows

The Explorer brings the manuscript's locus layer into a browsable form: 91 prioritised loci and their statistical locus representatives, each with its CTAS value, genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg q-value, cross-trait and eight-family profile, leave-one-trait-out stability, seven-tissue colocalisation, convergence with four prior comparator studies and FinnGen external support. The Loci browser ranks loci by CTAS and its effective breadth, filters by nested q threshold, family and evidence, and expands any locus to its full detail drawer.

Every field maps to a variable or analytical decision in the manuscript. Significance is defined by the genome-wide BH q-value, not by CTAS magnitude alone.

3

What coloc adds

Colocalisation tests whether a locus's cardiovascular association and local gene-expression signal are compatible with a single shared causal variant, across seven GTEx tissues. A locus is shown as colocalisation-supported when the posterior probability of a shared causal variant (PP4) reaches at least 0.5 in a tissue–trait context.

Colocalisation is analysed independently of CTAS and provides an orthogonal, tissue-resolved layer of regulatory support. PP4 supports a shared causal-variant model; it does not prove the causal gene, the causal variant or a mechanism, and absence of PP4 support is not evidence against a locus.

How CTAS is computed

From 16 traits to a calibrated gene-level statistic

Input panel and gene-level statistics. The primary analysis uses Pan-UK Biobank multi-ancestry meta-analysis P values for the 16 both-sex cardiovascular phenotypes listed in Table 1. For each trait, gene-level association P values are computed for 19,080 genes with MAGMA v1.10 (SNP-wise mean model; NCBI build 37.3 gene bodies on GRCh37/hg19). This yields, for each gene, a vector of 16 one-sided cross-trait evidence values.

Cross-trait dependence (transformed correlation matrix). The 16 × 16 trait correlation matrix is estimated from the genome-wide Spearman correlations ρS between the traits' gene-level evidence and converted to the Gaussian (Pearson) scale as R = 2 sin(πρS/6). Its minimum eigenvalue is 0.50, so R is positive definite and no eigenvalue floor was required. R parameterises the multivariate-normal working null used for both whitening and simulation.

ZCA-cor whitening and positive residual evidence. Each gene's clipped MAGMA P values are converted to upper-tail normal deviates z and whitened with the symmetric ZCA-cor whitener. Writing the eigendecomposition as R = VΛVT, where V contains the eigenvectors and Λ the eigenvalues, the whitener is W = VΛ−1/2VT and y = Wz (missing values contribute no positive evidence). Symmetric whitening removes the correlation expected under the fitted null without an order-dependent decomposition, keeping each whitened coordinate as close as possible to its original trait. Positive truncation occurs after whitening: for each whitened coordinate only positive evidence is retained and squared, wk = [max(0, yk)]2.

Magnitude, effective breadth and the CTAS product. The magnitude component is the size of the positive residual evidence, M = √(∑k wk). The effective breadth is the participation ratio of that evidence across decorrelated dimensions, PR = (∑k wk)2 / ∑k wk2, transformed as B = log2(1 + PR). CTAS is the product, CTAS = M × B, and is set to zero when no whitened coordinate is positive. Magnitude summarises the overall amount of positive residual evidence; breadth describes how widely it is spread. Whitened coordinates are mathematical mixtures of the traits, not named independent phenotypes, so individual coordinates are not given a direct biological interpretation.

Equivalently, CTAS = M × log2(1 + PR). CTAS is computed jointly from all 16 gene-level trait signals after correcting for their expected dependence. Magnitude measures total positive residual evidence, while PR measures its effective breadth across decorrelated axes. Because these axes are mixtures of the original traits, the named-trait profile provides biological context but cannot be divided into unique additive contributions to CTAS. This is a direct consequence of modelling the correlated trait panel jointly, not a limitation of the score: PR is an effective breadth across decorrelated axes and is not expected to equal the number of named traits or clinical families.

Calibration and false-discovery control

The primary empirical null contains 109 draws from the multivariate-normal working null with correlation R (fixed seed), each passed through the same whitening and CTAS calculation. Empirical P values use a plus-one correction, giving a direct resolution limit of about 1×10−9, and are adjusted across all 19,080 genes with the ordinary Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate procedure.

At genome-wide BH q < 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.001, CTAS prioritises 223 / 99 / 38 genes, which collapse to 91 / 46 / 21 prioritised loci. These are nested stringency sets, not biological categories. Primary membership is identical for null seeds 42, 1 and 2 at every threshold.

Raw-Spearman sensitivity. Using the raw Spearman matrix in place of the transformed correlation matrix changes little: the genome-wide rank correlation with the primary CTAS statistic is 0.99996.

Significance & nested q-value sets

Loci are grouped only by the most stringent genome-wide BH q threshold each one attains. The three sets are nested — every q < 0.001 locus is also a q < 0.01 and q < 0.05 locus — and they are stringency levels, not biological tiers. The landscape and the q tier column are coloured by this most-stringent threshold.

Loci, representatives & architecture

Locus formation and statistical representatives

Significant genes are assigned to a prespecified stable genomic-neighbourhood map built from MAGMA build-37.3 gene coordinates: overlapping gene intervals are joined and neighbours within 1 Mb merged. Regions are ordered deterministically and keep fixed locus identifiers, so the 91 final loci are not numbered contiguously. Within each locus, the statistical locus representative is the significant member gene with the highest CTAS. The representative is a reproducible statistical label for the locus; it is not automatically the causal gene, and neither it nor any curated biological-context gene is asserted to be causal.

Member-gene evidence. Each locus card opens on the highest-CTAS gene used as the statistical locus representative. Multi-gene loci also expose, through a “Compare member-gene evidence” disclosure, the individual CTAS components (CTAS, magnitude M, effective breadth PR, BH q) and raw 16-trait MAGMA profiles of every significant member gene. These gene-level statistics are shown as descriptive views of each gene’s own evidence and are not combined into a new locus-level score; nearby gene statistics are correlated and no locus-level null has been calibrated. Colocalisation and FinnGen results are attached to the representative/default evidence view and are not recomputed per member gene.

Descriptive trait and family profiles. For descriptive interpretation the 16 traits are grouped a priori into eight cardiovascular families: coronary (coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, angina), pressure (hypertension), arrhythmia (atrial fibrillation), heart failure (heart failure, cardiomyopathy), valve (aortic and mitral valve disease), aortic/vascular (aortic aneurysm, peripheral artery disease, atherosclerosis), cerebrovascular (ischaemic stroke, transient ischaemic attack) and thrombotic (phlebitis/thrombophlebitis, ICD-10 I80, and venous embolism/thrombosis, ICD-10 I82; the complete three-character categories, including all subcodes). A family is counted for a representative when at least one of its component traits reaches P < 0.01. The eight families are a descriptive coverage summary, not discrete clusters.

Robustness and external support

Leave-one-trait-out stability. Each of the 16 traits is removed in turn; a locus is ultra-stable when it is retained at q < 0.05 in all 16 leave-one-trait-out runs (47 of the 91 loci), meaning no single trait is responsible for its inclusion. These are precomputed sensitivity analyses that used raw-Spearman working matrices and 106-draw nulls, rebased to the final 91 loci; they were not rerun under the primary 109-draw analysis.

Colocalisation. All 91 statistical locus representatives were targeted for colocalisation with coloc.abf against cis-eQTL statistics from seven GTEx tissues in the eQTL Catalogue — heart left ventricle, heart atrial appendage, aorta, coronary artery, whole blood, liver and kidney cortex — using European-ancestry Pan-UK Biobank GWAS effect fields. Of the 91, 87 were assessed and 4 were not assessed: HOXD13, HDGFL1 and KCNK4 have no cis-eQTL for the representative gene in any of the seven tissues, and ATP6V1G2 lies in the excluded MHC. Not-assessed loci are missing evidence, never negative results. Among the 87 assessed loci, 17 had strong PP4 support (PP4 ≥ 0.8), 14 moderate PP4 support, 25 PP3-only evidence and 31 neither, so 31/91 loci (31/87 assessed) are colocalisation-supported, across 150 tissue–trait cells at PP4 ≥ 0.5 (83 at PP4 ≥ 0.8). PP4 ≥ 0.5 supports a shared-causal-variant model; it does not prove a causal gene, a causal variant or a mechanism. PP3 indicates both signals are present but more consistent with distinct local variants.

Prior comparator studies. Convergence is assessed against four fixed studies that differ in method and reporting unit, each matched on its native unit — Qiao et al. (interval overlap), Liu et al. (gene symbol), Foley et al. (candidate-variant coordinate containment) and PLEIO (index-variant coordinate containment). At q < 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.001 they match 19 / 15 / 8 (Qiao), 31 / 22 / 13 (Liu), 12 / 11 / 9 (Foley) and 48 / 28 / 17 (PLEIO) loci; the four-study union covers 58 / 33 / 17. This is convergence, not formal confirmation. “Not in 4 comparators” means unmatched by all four; it is not a claim of biological novelty.

FinnGen external support. CTAS genes were looked up in FinnGen release 12 across ten cardiovascular endpoints (atrial fibrillation, angina, coronary atherosclerosis, cardiomyopathy, heart failure, hypertension, myocardial infarction, stroke, transient ischaemic attack and venous thromboembolism; the FinnGen venous thromboembolism endpoint I9_VTE is defined by FinnGen and is distinct from the Pan-UK Biobank ICD-10 I82 trait). Endpoints were grouped into six clinical families (coronary, arrhythmia, myocardial, pressure, cerebrovascular and venous) so that correlated endpoints, such as CAD, MI and angina, are not counted as independent support; a family is supported when any member endpoint has gene-level P < 0.01. The proportion of genes supported in at least three of the six families increases from 49% at q < 0.05 (109/223) to 57% at q < 0.01 (56/99) and 61% at q < 0.001 (23/38), against 6% (1,154/18,701) for evaluable background genes with q ≥ 0.05. Endpoint-based counts are a sensitivity analysis in the supplementary table. FinnGen summary statistics (GRCh38) were converted to GRCh37 before MAGMA gene analysis. The locus drawer shows separate representative-gene FinnGen support: all 91 statistical locus representatives were evaluable (a representative absent from FinnGen would be shown as not evaluable, never as zero). CTAS was not rerun or recalculated in FinnGen; this is external support and does not re-estimate the statistic.

How to read a locus card

Clicking any row in the Loci browser opens a detail drawer. The fields and badges shown there are defined below.

Representative gene
The statistical locus representative: the significant member gene with the highest CTAS in the locus. It labels the locus and is not automatically the causal gene.
CTAS
The CTAS statistic for the statistical locus representative: magnitude × breadth of positive whitened residual evidence. Higher values indicate more, and more broadly distributed, cross-trait evidence beyond the correlated-panel background. The table bar scales to the maximum CTAS across the 91 loci.
q tier / BH q
The most stringent genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg q threshold the locus attains (q < 0.001, q < 0.01 or q < 0.05). Significance is defined by BH q, not by CTAS magnitude alone. The sets are nested stringency levels.
Effective breadth (PR)
The main-table breadth column: the participation ratio, PR = (∑k wk)2 / ∑k wk2, i.e. the effective breadth of the representative gene's positive evidence across the decorrelated (whitened) axes. These axes are mixtures of the original traits, so PR is not a count of named traits and is not expected to equal a number of clinical families. It enters the score as CTAS = M × log2(1 + PR).
Family labels (three complementary views)
The Explorer shows up to three cardiovascular-family labels, which describe different information and are not competing classifications. All refer to the statistical representative gene; none assigns causality, and none is aggregated across a locus's member genes.
  • Raw-profile leading family (the main-table “Raw-profile family” column and the card headline): the family carrying the strongest evidence in the representative's original MAGMA profile, before dependence correction. A descriptive summary of the raw trait profile — not the CTAS selection rule. Loci resting on a single nominally significant trait, or with no family reaching about 30% of the raw evidence, are shown as “Broad — no dominant family” rather than forced into a family.
  • Leading family after dependence correction (secondary, in the drawer): the family carrying the largest share of the representative's positive residual evidence after the fitted cross-trait dependence has been discounted. A descriptive projection of the whitened profile, not a separate significance test.
  • Prior cardiovascular context (shown with the biological annotation, where available): the cardiovascular context previously associated with the gene or region in the curated literature — contextual evidence, not a CTAS-derived result.
The full cross-family profile, including the effective family breadth (Family Neff), is a separate descriptive summary in the drawer and is distinct from the CTAS effective breadth PR.
Why can the family labels differ? Raw-profile leading family summarises where the representative gene's original association evidence is strongest. Leading family after dependence correction describes the largest residual family contribution after correlated evidence has been discounted. Prior cardiovascular context summarises existing knowledge about the gene or region. These are complementary views, not competing classifications.
Raw trait / family summary (drawer)
Descriptive context shown in the locus drawer: the number of the 16 cardiovascular traits, and of the eight trait families, reaching gene-level P < 0.01 (and P < 0.001) for the representative, in canonical panel order. These are a compact summary of the signal profile for interpretation, not additive components of CTAS. The signal-profile chart shows all 16 traits in the fixed canonical panel order; a very significant trait whose MAGMA P underflowed to zero is drawn at the ceiling and labelled as a bound (P ≤ 1×10−300), not an exact value.
Colocalisation (PP4 / PP3)
The strongest tissue–trait colocalisation for the representative. PP4 ≥ 0.5 supports a shared causal-variant model; PP3 marks regional overlap consistent with distinct local variants. "No PP4 support" and "Not assessed" are kept distinct. Colocalisation is not proof of a causal variant, gene or mechanism.
Representative-gene FinnGen support
Whether the statistical locus representative was evaluable in FinnGen R12 and, if so, how many of the six clinical families (and ten cardiovascular endpoints) reach gene-level P < 0.01, with the supported families and endpoints named. Unavailable representatives are shown as not evaluable, never as zero. This is a locus-level view; the gene-level FinnGen headline above describes all CTAS genes. CTAS was not rerun in FinnGen.
Ultra-stable
Retained at q < 0.05 in all 16 leave-one-trait-out sensitivity runs (raw-Spearman working matrices, 106-draw nulls) — no single trait is responsible for the locus's inclusion.
Prior support / Not in 4 comparators
Which of Qiao, Liu, Foley and PLEIO report the locus (shown in the drawer), or none of the four. "Not in 4 comparators" means unmatched by this multi-trait comparator set — not a claim of biological novelty; many such loci carry established single-trait associations.
Canonical CVD
Shown only where inherited prior-locus curation or the Table 3 curated evidence establishes the locus as an established cardiovascular locus. It is not inferred from the trait profile alone.
Biological annotation, evidence context & sources
Each locus carries a concise biological note (what the representative encodes, prior cardiovascular genetics or function where established, and what CTAS adds), clickable sources (PubMed/DOI for genetics or function; NCBI Gene/UniProt for basic molecular function), and an evidence-context label. Those labels — Established cross-CVD, Established CVD, CVD-relevant biology and Emerging cross-trait — describe the strength and breadth of prior evidence at the locus; they are not CTAS discovery tiers and not causal-gene classifications. Where a nearby member gene carries the clearer prior biology it is shown as a separate biological context gene. Throughout, the statistical representative labels the locus and neither it nor any context, comparator-matched or colocalised gene is asserted to be the causal gene; direct cardiovascular-disease genetics and cardiovascular risk-factor genetics are treated as distinct.

Prior comparator studies

Convergence is assessed against four fixed cross-trait cardiovascular studies, each matched on its native unit. A locus with Prior support matches at least one (the four-study union: 58 of 91 loci); Not in 4 comparators denotes unmatched by all four (33 loci), which is convergent-support absence, not biological novelty.

Qiao et al. Multi-trait cardiovascular GWAS
The 38 multi-trait cardiovascular loci reported by Qiao et al. are matched by direct overlap between their GRCh37 intervals and the CTAS locus intervals, with no added flank (19 of 91 loci).
Liu et al. Ten cardiovascular diseases
The unique genes reported by Liu et al. across ten cardiovascular diseases are matched by HGNC-normalised gene symbol to any q < 0.05 CTAS gene within each locus (31 of 91 loci).
Foley et al. HyPrColoc, coronary heart disease
The 43 coronary-heart-disease colocalisation regions reported by Foley et al. are matched by containment of their candidate-variant coordinates within the CTAS locus intervals (12 of 91 loci).
Lee et al. PLEIO, cross-phenotype index variants
The 625 index variants reported by PLEIO are matched by coordinate containment within the CTAS locus intervals (48 of 91 loci). Because phenotype coverage, methods and matching units differ across the four studies, these comparisons are read as literature convergence rather than strict confirmation.
